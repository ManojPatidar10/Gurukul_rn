Gamified learning that runs on real school life

Students already love games. Smart Gurukul gives them one inside school, where the points come from showing up, doing well and taking part, not from screen time.

In short: every student in Smart Gurukul has one XP score. It goes up with attendance, assessment results, quiz wins and teacher-awarded participation. That XP drives daily streaks, weekly leagues and school-wide House Wars, and students can play live Battle Room quizzes and 1v1 Arena challenges built from their teachers’ own questions.

The Game Hub

One XP score. Many ways to earn it.

XP is the single currency behind everything, so the game feels like one coherent system rather than five bolted-on features.

  • Attendance: every day marked present earns XP and grows the streak.
  • Assessments: recorded results earn XP.
  • Quiz wins: Arena and Battle Room victories earn XP.
  • Participation: teachers can award XP and house points for effort in class.
  • Streak bonuses: milestones reward consistency.

Why gamification works in a school app

Most school apps are opened by the office and ignored by students. When attendance, results and participation turn into XP, students have their own reason to open the app, and every time they do, they also see their attendance, results and school notices.

It also rewards the things schools already care about. A streak is built by showing up. League rank comes from effort across the week. House Wars turn individual effort into team spirit, which is familiar to every Indian school with a house system.

What teachers control

  • The questions: Arena and Battle Room quizzes use questions teachers author for their own subjects and classes.
  • Recognition: teachers can award participation XP and house points for effort in class.
  • Houses: the school sets up its own houses and names.

FAQ

Gamification: common questions

What is gamified learning in a school app?

It means adding game mechanics such as points, streaks, leagues and competitions to school activities. In Smart Gurukul, students earn XP for attendance, assessment results, quiz wins and participation, and use it to climb leagues and win House Wars.

Does gamification distract students from studies?

In Smart Gurukul the game rewards school behaviour (attending, doing well in assessments, answering subject quizzes written by teachers), so playing it means doing more of what school already asks.

Who writes the quiz questions?

Teachers. Arena and Battle Room quizzes use a question bank that teachers author for their own subject and class.

Can we use our school’s own houses?

Yes. The school sets up its own houses, and student XP plus teacher spot-awards feed the House Wars standings.
