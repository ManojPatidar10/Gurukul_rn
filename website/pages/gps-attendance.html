Teacher attendance by GPS, with no machine to buy

Teachers check in from their own phone when they reach school. There’s no biometric machine to buy, queue at or repair, and student attendance takes the class teacher two taps.

In short: the school saves its location and a radius once (100 metres by default). When a teacher taps Mark my attendance, the phone sends its GPS position and our server, not the phone, checks the distance to the school. At school, they’re marked present. Too far away, the check-in is refused and shows how far they are.

How it works

Four steps, set up once

  1. Save the school’s location. The principal taps “use my current location” at school and sets the radius.
  2. Teachers check in on arrival. One tap, and the phone shares its GPS position.
  3. The server decides. Distance is worked out on our server from the school’s saved location, so the phone can’t simply declare itself “at school”.
  4. History fills itself in. Every teacher gets a monthly calendar of present, absent, late and half-day.

FAQ

GPS attendance: common questions

How does GPS attendance work for teachers?

The school saves its location and a radius. A teacher taps “Mark my attendance” on arrival, the phone sends its GPS position, and our server checks the distance to the school. At school, they are marked present; too far away, the check-in is refused.

Can teachers fake their location?

The decision is made on our server from the school’s saved location, never by the phone, and every check-in’s location and accuracy are recorded. As with any GPS system, someone using special location-spoofing tools is harder to rule out than with a biometric machine.

What radius should a school use?

The default is 100 metres from the saved point. Larger campuses can increase it.

Do we need a biometric machine?

No. GPS check-in uses teachers’ own phones, so there is no hardware to buy or repair.
